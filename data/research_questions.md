# Research Questions

*The questions that drove the study. The three original questions framed the project; a second round of
"extended" questions was generated across disciplinary lenses (intellectual history, network science, book
history, digital humanities) once the graph existed. The reports answer them; the column on the right points to
where.*

---

## The three original questions

| | Question | Answered in |
|---|---|---|
| **RQ1** | Whom did the Hida treat as **central** in the Jewish "bookshelf" of his era, and is "central" one thing or several? | Ch. 1 (roles) |
| **RQ2** | What **typed relations** (teacher–student, citation, dispute, authorship, family, contemporary…) structure that world, and what do they reveal about **transmission and influence**? | Ch. 2–3 |
| **RQ3** | What does the Hida's **editorial structure** — whom he places inside whose entry — reveal as **latent knowledge**? | Ch. 4 |

## The extended questions

Grouped as in the study. Each was chosen to exploit what makes this graph distinctive: a genuine **multiplex**
(three tie-types over the same nodes), **books as first-class nodes**, and the **provenance stamp** on every stated
relation (which entry it came from).

**Who mattered, and in what way** *(Ch. 1)*
- Do a scholar's four centralities sort the canon into interpretable **roles** — *authority* (cited ≫ citing) vs.
  *compiler/transmitter* (citing ≫ cited) vs. *balanced* vs. *connector*?
- **The silences:** which heavily-invoked authorities did the Hida never grant an entry — and what does that
  inclusion boundary say about who counts as a "great one"?

**How Torah was transmitted** *(Ch. 2)*
- How deep do **teacher–student lineages** run, and who are the **load-bearing masters** whose removal fragments
  the chain of tradition?
- Did authority pass by **blood or by discipleship** (kinship vs. pedagogy)?
- Do the automatically-found **circles** correspond to recognizable **schools** (by the kind of tie that binds them)?
- What **recurring patterns** (motifs) govern transmission — do teacher-chains form cliques; do students cite
  their teachers more than chance?

**The world of books** *(Ch. 3)*
- Does the Hida locate authority in **texts or in people** (the mix of citation "modes")?
- Do books form a **genealogy of texts** (commentary-on / based-on / gloss-on), and what are its foundational roots?
- Can a **book outshine its author** (author-vs-book "eclipse")?
- Which **books travel together** (co-citation), and who are the **bridge-scholars** across textual traditions?

**The Hida's hand** *(Ch. 4)*
- Which relations does he assert only **tacitly** (by placement, never stated)?
- **Where does he stage controversy** — in a disputant's own entry or a third party's?
- Does **entry length** track a figure's importance, or reveal the author's disproportionate **attention**?
- Is the supplement (*Kuntres Aharon*) **gap-repair or reinforcement**?
- Are his stated relations **about the entry's subject** or **narrated between third parties**?
- Does the Hida **write himself and his family** into the graph beyond their citation warrant?

**A mystical lineage** *(Ch. 5)*
- Is the **soul-root (gilgul)** overlay a re-description of ordinary transmission, or an **independent** esoteric
  order laid over the canon's most central figures?

**The geography of the book** *(Ch. 6)*
- Is the geography of **making books** (printing) the same as the geography of **living Torah** (rabbinate,
  residence, death)?
- Do the **circles have homelands**? Did scholars **move** (origin vs. death place; migration to the Land of Israel)?
